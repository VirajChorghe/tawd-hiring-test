# TAWD Creative Director Review (final step before the team sees mockups)

A separate reviewer agent with fresh eyes. It did not build the mockups, and it reviews all three side by side. It runs **after** the mechanical QA checks (contrast, headings, links, render bugs) and **before** anything reaches the PM.

## Process

1. **Review:** score each mockup (1-5) on the eleven questions below and write specific, fixable notes. Each note names the section, the problem, and the change.
2. **Show or improve:** a mockup averaging **4.5 or above** (with no must-fix) is shown to the PM as it is. Under that, the builder applies the notes once, to the sections they name.
3. **Re-check:** the reviewer scores it again and confirms each note was addressed; mechanical QA still passes.
4. **Present or hold:** 4.5 or above is presented to the PM with a short review note. Still under 4.5 is **held**: not shown to the PM or the client, with the reason; an admin can show it anyway. There is no rebuild (Jason, 8 October 2026: "I want to see 4.5/5 or above on the 1st go").

Mark like VML's web designer: 4.5 means it can go to the client without a section being redone by hand.

## The eleven questions

1. **5-second test:** could a stressed visitor tell what the business does, where, and how to call within 5 seconds?
2. **Direction fit:** does it clearly feel like its chosen direction and not like the other two?
3. **Signature:** is there one memorable element, and does it carry proof or usefulness rather than decoration?
4. **Conversion path:** is there a call to action in the hero, mid-page and before the footer? Is the phone never more than one scroll away?
5. **Helpfulness over claims:** where facts are missing, does the page earn trust with genuinely useful content (what to do right now, what happens next) instead of empty boxes? Optional facts the intake doesn't have are simply absent: no card, line or section for them.
6. **Placeholders only where required:** is there a placeholder only where a required fact is missing, designed as a skeleton of the real thing? A placeholder or section for an optional or unasked fact scores 2 at most (8 October 2026).
7. **Pop and rhythm:** does the page pop? The brand colour carries whole bands, not just buttons; backgrounds and density vary; the accent is in every screen-height of scroll. A dull, washed-out page scores 2 at most.
8. **Above the fold:** is the most important element (the phone, or the signature) visible without scrolling at 1440x900?
9. **Client choice** (added 27 September 2026): would a real client in this trade choose this over their current site? Judged against the reference sites the client chose (their screenshots come with the review). Passing checks isn't the same as being sellable.
10. **Pictures fit** (8 October 2026): does every picture belong in its section and show what it is about, and is every icon the right one? A forced or irrelevant picture, an empty frame or a wrong icon scores 2 at most.
11. **Ready to send** (8 October 2026): would VML's web designer send it to the client exactly as it is, without redoing a section by hand? Anything they would fix themselves scores 3 at most.

## Rules

- The reviewer never adds facts, claims or content the intake form did not supply. General safety advice (for example, where a water shut-off valve usually is) is allowed, but it is flagged for the content team to verify.
- Improvements stay inside the chosen direction and the locked brand inputs.
- **A flat, image-poor or unbranded page is a must-fix** (few or no real pictures, placeholder frames where photos belong, none of the client's brand colours). It scores 2 at most on Signature and Client choice, and TAWD also measures it without AI, so it fails the review whatever the scores say.
- The reference sites are the bar for finish, photography and structure. A concept never takes their names, words, logos or photos.
- The review note is shown to the team in the project workspace, with the reviewer's scores before and after.
